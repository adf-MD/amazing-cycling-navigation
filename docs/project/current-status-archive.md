### Field test, 10 September 2026 — installed-iPhone route ride and Planning session: seven follow-up findings

Context: the installed iPhone Home Screen PWA, portrait. The report did not record which deployed version was in use, so none is claimed here; item 106's own acceptance had been reported earlier the same day. The ride included a substantial period during which ACN had been suspended and connectivity had been absent. Seven findings came out of the session, each filed as its own backlog item so that the visual-clean-up queue would not absorb evidence-led work. Observations and inferences are deliberately left as first recorded, even where the eventual implementation found something different; the authoritative execution order lives in the root [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md)'s queue index.

- **1. Progress matched to the wrong part of overlapping geometry after a long suspension — a correctness observation, no root cause claimed ([item 107](history/items-104-109.md#item-107)).** **Observed:** during a route ride, after ACN had been suspended and connectivity had been absent for a substantial period, the resumed position appeared to be matched to the wrong part of overlapping or closely adjacent route geometry. **Not claimed, deliberately:** it is **not** recorded that ACN failed to recover — the rider may have seen automatic recovery, or may have pressed the existing retry control, and which of those happened was not captured. The prolonged absence of connectivity is **not** classified as a defect; riding through it is what the application is designed for. **Inference (the user's, not established):** that suspension-recovery and overlapping-geometry candidate selection were the two places worth inspecting. Item 107 treated that as a starting point, not a diagnosis. It shipped in `0.4.24`, finding a reachable defect in the second of those two places; see its entry above and its [history record](history/items-104-109.md#item-107).
- **2. The climb cue and the delayed-imagery explanation overlapped at the top of the map — with an approved product decision attached ([item 108](history/items-104-109.md#item-108)).** **Observed:** the screenshot showed the "Climb active" cue and the delayed-map-imagery explanation occupying the same top portion of the map and overlapping one another; separately, the cue used three lines and left excessive empty space. **Expected, not a defect:** the missing base imagery during the prolonged offline period; nothing here is a report against the imagery pipeline's own correctness. **Approved product behaviour (a decision, not an observation):** during **both Route riding and Free roam**, all map-imagery status belongs in the existing top riding-status card rather than in an overlay over the map; a transient slow load may add a compact row, a retryable or terminal problem transitions that row to the appropriate unavailable state and retains **Retry map imagery** where retry is meaningful; the row disappears when imagery recovers, with **no** fixed disappearance timeout that could conceal a continuing failure; and the card must not redundantly repeat that the device is offline. Both the in-map delayed-imagery banner **and** the in-map initial-loading message are suppressed during active Route riding and Free roam. Planning and other contexts without the riding-status card retain their existing in-map explanation. **Route riding may say that the route and position remain visible; Free roam must mention only the position, because no route is active there.** **Approved outcome for the climb cue**, stated as an outcome rather than one CSS arrangement: preserve "Climb active", the remaining distance and **View climb** without truncating meaningful text; make the cue materially more compact at ordinary supported portrait widths; allow accessible reflow at enlarged browser text; avoid covering an unnecessary amount of the route ahead; do not overlap map controls, attribution, other live notices or the imagery status; and retain usable touch targets and existing climb behaviour. Item 108 shipped in `0.4.25`; see its entry above and its [history record](history/items-104-109.md#item-108), including the finding that the cue's own mechanism was not what "three lines" implied.
- **3. The Planning waypoint marker sits on the "Add waypoint here" control — a confirmed presentation defect ([item 109](history/items-104-109.md#item-109)).** **Observed and confirmed:** the installed-iPhone screenshot shows the numbered waypoint marker or badge visually sitting on the upper edge of the "Add waypoint here" control — a confirmed presentation defect, not a suspicion. **No fix was asserted** at the time, deliberately, because the marker's stacking value is documented as paired with the distance badges'.
